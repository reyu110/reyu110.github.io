How does it work?

Three loops: find the audience, watch Google, work with creators

Each loop has four stages. Every source runs on its own, so one failing never costs you the rest.

Communities and creators
  1. Define

    Paste a link. AI or the built-in engine suggests keywords. You edit and save.

  2. Discover

    Each keyword searches YouTube and, through Bright Data, TikTok, Instagram and X.

  3. Rank

    Off-topic results drop out. The rest get a score within their own platform.

  4. Track and show

    Every run is saved with its date. The dashboard shows the latest per platform, trends, and an AI-written target market.

Google rankings
  1. Collect

    Each term is searched on Google, your pages are audited, and Google's update feed is read.

  2. Detect

    Today is compared with the median of recent checks. Only changes that persist, or are severe, alert.

  3. Advise

    Every alert gets causes and fixes from Google's guidance. Groq adds advice from your own page and rivals.

  4. Show

    The SEO tab with a fix queue and keyword ideas, a scheduled run and a macOS alert. History is kept for trends.

Working with creators
  1. Shortlist

    Pick creators from the ranked list, or add someone by hand. Each sits in one stage, and the day it began is kept.

  2. Reach out

    Groq drafts a message from your offer and website, and emails found in public bios are offered. You send it yourself.

  3. Take applications

    Import a CSV or paste rows. Check each applicant’s real follower count, then accept or reject.

  4. Fund and follow

    Record each grant. Funded creators are read from their platforms daily, so their audience then and now sits beside the niche’s.

How is it built? · reference

Twenty small files, one job each

About 5,420 lines of app code and 3,100 lines of checks. The dashboard calls everything else, and the scoring and SEO rules are pure functions that never touch the network.

How a score is made

Each metric becomes a percentile rank inside its own platform, then the three are blended.

If a platform does not supply a metric, the score ignores it and rescales the other weights instead of scoring it zero.

Twelve design decisions

Rank within each platform
Followers mean different things on different platforms. A percentile inside one platform stops a single huge number from taking over.
Median, not average
One viral video pushed a 1,240-subscriber channel to #2. Using the median views per video moved it to #5.
Relevance gate
A community stays only if it mentions at least 15% of your keywords as whole words, and none of your excludes.
Dated snapshots
Every run is stored with its date, so trends and “what changed” need no extra code.
Baseline, not wobble
Today is compared with the median of the last 7 checks. A warning needs a second check below baseline; a first sighting shows as “watch”.
A failed lookup is not a lost ranking
If a Google search errors or returns nothing, no row is saved. An outage can never look like a drop.
Sources fail alone
A missing key shows as skipped and an error as failed. The other sources still save.
Capped and local
At most 20 profiles per platform per run. The dashboard serves only your own machine. Keys stay in .env.
AI kept to the evidence
Groq sees only your page, the rivals' titles and the communities found. Code enforces the rest: rewrites stay within length limits and never copy a rival, target-market segments name only communities that were found, and a message draft leaves a gap where a personal touch belongs.
The tool records, you act
A message draft is never sent and a grant never moves money. Every step you take is stored with its date, so reply rates and decision times are worked out, not typed in.
Say where a number came from
An applicant’s audience is marked self-reported unless the daily runs track that creator, and a comparison with the niche needs at least three other creators.
Cost shown before it is spent
Each run shows an estimate first, and a monthly meter counts only what Bright Data actually billed.
